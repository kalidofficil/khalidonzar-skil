# 30-day plan

Every action names the hiring gap it closes. Nothing here is activity for its own sake.

Assumed capacity: ~15 hours a week alongside other commitments. If you have more, add
applications and case studies — not certificates.

---

## WEEK 1 — Stop the bleeding, then rebuild the profile

**Day 1 — 45 minutes, do this before anything else**

| Action | Gap it closes |
|---|---|
| Delete both certifications from LinkedIn | Removes a verifiable false claim — the single highest-risk item in your materials |
| Fix your name to "Khalid Ounzar"; capitalise all company and school names | Name-matching across LinkedIn, resume and portfolio; basic credibility |
| Reconcile every Experience date against real records; correct durations and set employment types | Closes the ~4-years-implied vs ~18-months-described gap before any recruiter sees it |
| Confirm to yourself: do **not** merge this repo to `main` | Prevents publishing $8.4M / 4.1× ROAS / placeholder testimonials under your name |

**Days 2–3**
- Paste the new headline (Option 1) and About. Turn on Open to Work with the four target titles. → *Makes you findable under titles recruiters actually search, and stops reading as a freelancer.*
- Re-pin Top Skills to Meta Ads / Performance Marketing / E-commerce. → *Your strongest channel currently isn't in the three skills shown under your name.*
- Rewrite all Experience entries from `01-LINKEDIN-REWRITE.md`. → *Replaces job-description bullets with decisions.*
- Customise your LinkedIn URL; add phone; confirm which email you monitor. → *Recruiters currently cannot reliably contact you, and the profile URL looks unfinished.*

**Days 4–5**
- Gather every campaign screenshot. For each, record: date range, attribution setting, account, your role, **what the Purchase event fired on**. → *This is the fact everything else depends on.*
- Answer the eight questions in `00-AUDIT.md` §2. → *Unblocks both resumes.*

**Days 6–7**

**Week 1 output:** honest profile, no false claims, one sendable resume.

## WEEK 2 — Build the proof that replaces experience you don't have

**Days 8–10 — Case study 1: "What a $0.56 cost per purchase actually means in COD"**
One page. Screenshot with date range and attribution visible, the objective, what you
tested, what you changed and why, the result with its correct label, what you'd do
differently. → *Closes the "campaign metrics without commercial context" gap and converts
your most suspicious-looking number into your best credibility signal.*

**Days 11–12 — Case study 2: creative test breakdown (the 61.08% hook rate)**
The hook, what it beat, why you thought it would win, the link CTR result, what you tested
next. → *Closes "creative work without strategy or testing rationale" — the single reason
the creative strategist verdict is "not yet".*

**Days 13–14 — Featured section**
Upload both case studies as PDFs, in the order given in `01-LINKEDIN-REWRITE.md`. → *The
only place a recruiter sees your actual work without leaving your profile.*

**Also this week:** start GA4 certification (~6 hours). → *The most-assumed skill you
visibly lack, and the cheapest of your gaps to close.*

**Week 2 output:** two case studies live in Featured. Verdict A moves from borderline to yes.

## WEEK 3 — Apply properly, and finish the creative artefacts

**Applications — 10 targeted, not 50 scattergun**
Split roughly: 5 UAE e-commerce/D2C brands, 3 small-to-mid performance agencies (5–40
staff), 2 GCC COD/dropship operators. Titles: Media Buyer (Junior), Performance Marketing
Executive, Paid Social Executive, Digital Marketing Executive (Paid).
For each: tailor the summary line to the posting, and link the relevant case study.
→ *Targeting beats volume at junior level, where a generic application is indistinguishable
from a hundred others.*

**Days 15–17:** finish GA4 cert; write one creative brief retrospectively. → *Closes the
"no brief artefacts" gap that blocks the creative track.*

**Days 18–19:** storyboard-to-final comparison for one project. → *Proves you plan rather
than improvise.*

**Days 20–21:** rehearse both 45–60 second intros out loud until they are not recited. Run
the ten interview questions from `06-PORTFOLIO-AND-INTERVIEW.md`, especially the AOV
question and the budget question. → *These two decide your interviews.*

**Networking — 5 personalised messages, no templates**
Target UAE performance marketers and agency owners. Reference something specific they
posted. Ask one real question. Do not ask for a job in a first message. → *UAE hiring runs
heavily on referral; cold applications are the weakest channel you have.*

## WEEK 4 — Content, iterate, second application round

**LinkedIn content — 2 posts, not more**
Both drawn from work you have already done:
1. **"What a purchase event means in COD e-commerce."** Your case study, rewritten as a post.
   *Purpose: it demonstrates the exact analytical judgement the recruiter verdict says you
   need to prove, in front of the people hiring for it. It is not content for engagement —
   it is a public artefact of your thinking that costs nothing extra to produce.*
2. **"The hook that held 61% of viewers, and the two that didn't."**
   *Purpose: the creative-strategy evidence your portfolio lacks, in the format that travels
   furthest on LinkedIn.*

**Days 22–24:** post 1; respond to every comment. Follow up on week 3 applications (one
short message, seven days after applying).

**Days 25–27:** 10 more targeted applications, adjusted for what week 3 taught you. Post 2.

**Days 28–30:** review and reset. Third case study if the first two are done properly. Start
Meta Blueprint or Google Ads Search certification — **only now**, because a certificate
matters far less than the case studies and should not have displaced them. → *You are
already advertising Google Ads; earning it retires that liability.*

## Sustainable cadence past day 30

- **Posting:** 1–2 posts a week, every one drawn from real work. Stop if you find yourself
  posting generic marketing advice — it actively signals inexperience, because everyone at
  your level does it and nobody senior does.
- **Networking:** 5 personalised messages a week. Never a template.
- **Applications:** 8–10 targeted a week. If you are sending more than 15, you are not
  tailoring them.
- **Case studies:** one a month until you have five.

---

## Tracking

Track activity you control and outcomes you don't. Weekly, in a spreadsheet.

**Controllable:** relevant applications sent · personalised outreach messages · case studies
completed · posts published · interview questions rehearsed

**Outcomes:** recruiter replies · screening calls · first interviews · final interviews ·
offers · profile views and search appearances (LinkedIn analytics) · Featured document views

**What the numbers should tell you:**
- **Applications up, replies near zero →** the profile or resume is the problem, not volume. Stop applying and fix it.
- **Replies coming, screening calls not →** your resume and LinkedIn disagree, or the seniority mismatch is showing.
- **Screening calls, no interviews →** the intro and the budget question. Go back to Part 2 and Part 3.
- **Interviews, no offers →** you are close. It is probably the tracking answer or a claim you could not defend.

Review every Sunday. Change one thing at a time — same discipline you apply to a campaign.
