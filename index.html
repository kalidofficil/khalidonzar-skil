Campaign analysis

What case C actually says.

Anyone can screenshot a good week. The value is in naming which part of the funnel broke.

01

The creative worked. The promise did not carry.

A 61.08% hook rate says the first frames earned attention at scale. A 2.29% link CTR against that says the ad never converted attention into intent — people watched, understood it was an advert, and stayed.

02

Cheap reach bought the wrong attention.

A $0.46 CPM is unusually cheap, and cheap delivery usually means broad, low-intent placements. Volume arrived; buying intent did not. The $2.14 cost per reported purchase against case A's $0.56 is the same story told in money.

03

Move the ask closer to the hook.

Shorten the distance between the hook and the offer, put the price and the guarantee in the creative rather than on the page, and tighten placements even at a higher CPM. Case A and case B are what that discipline looks like once applied.

04

The honest limit of this analysis.

These are platform-reported numbers. I can evidence what Ads Manager recorded; I cannot evidence what was delivered, confirmed or kept after returns, so I do not claim it.

Concepts

Two briefs, worked through.

Neither has run. They show how I think about a brief before any money is spent — not results.

Campaign concept

Hair salon

A local service business with a fixed chair count. The constraint is not reach, it is calendar capacity: an ad that fills Saturday and leaves Tuesday empty has not worked.

  • Radius tight enough that travel time never becomes the objection
  • Offer built on a first-visit service, not a discount on everything
  • Creative shot in the chair — the result, not the premises
  • Judged on cost per booking, rebooking at 8 weeks, and weekday fill as its own line
Sample strategy

Real estate

Long consideration, high value, and a lead a human has to call. The question is not how to get leads cheaply — it is how to get leads an agent will still return calls to in week three.

  • Browsing intent and viewing intent split, never one blended cost per lead
  • Qualification inside the form, accepting a higher cost per lead on purpose
  • Creative leading with the specific unit, price band and handover date
  • Judged on cost per qualified lead and cost per viewing, agreed before launch
