reported vs real

The one thing

A good cost per purchase is not a profit.

Ads Manager counts a sale the moment somebody taps the button. In cash on delivery, the money is decided much later: on the confirmation call, and then at the customer's door.

Refusals in Morocco run between fifteen and thirty percent. Skip the confirmation calls and returns climb past forty. The cash itself arrives a week or two after the van does. So the figure glowing in your ad account is a claim, not a result.

The distance between that claim and your bank balance is where I work.

the objections

The questions people actually ask

How do I know you will not just burn my budget like the last one?

Start with the audit. Before anything is launched or changed, I read what you already have and tell you what I see. If the honest read is that ads are not your problem, I will say that instead of selling you a retainer.

My ad account looks good and I am still not making money. Why?

In cash on delivery that is normal. Ads Manager counts an order at the tap. Your profit is decided on the confirmation call and at the customer's door. Until confirmation rate and delivery rate sit in the same view as cost per purchase, the account will keep looking better than the bank.

Where are you, and does that matter?

I am based in Dubai. The cash-on-delivery work is mostly Moroccan and regional accounts, because that is where I know the delivery reality on the ground. Everything else, the Meta buying and the funnel work, travels anywhere.

What do you need from me to start?

Read access to the ad account, whatever order data you have, and one honest conversation about the offer and the margins. If the margins are thin, I would rather know on day one than find out in month three.

Are you an agency?

No. There is no account manager between us and no junior running your campaigns. That also means I take on a small number of accounts at a time.
