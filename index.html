Campaign analysis

What case C actually says.

Reading a campaign is the skill. Anyone can screenshot a good week; the value is in naming which part of the funnel broke.

The reading

The creative worked. The promise did not carry.

A 61.08% hook rate says the first frames earned attention at scale. A 2.29% link CTR against that says the ad never converted attention into intent — people watched, understood it was an advert, and stayed.

The likely cause

Cheap reach bought the wrong attention.

A $0.46 CPM is unusually cheap. Cheap delivery usually means broad, low-intent placements. Volume arrived; buying intent did not. The $2.14 cost per reported purchase against case A’s $0.56 is the same story told in money.

What I changed

Move the ask closer to the hook.

Shorten the distance between the hook and the offer, put the price and the guarantee in the creative rather than on the page, and tighten placements even at a higher CPM. Case A and case B are what that discipline looks like once applied.

The honest limit of this analysis. These are platform-reported numbers. I can evidence what Ads Manager recorded; I cannot evidence what was delivered, confirmed or kept after returns, so I do not claim it.

Concepts

Two briefs, worked through.

Neither of these has run. They are here to show how I think about a brief before any money is spent — not as results.

Concept — not a client result

Hair salon — campaign concept

A local service business with a fixed chair count. The constraint is not reach, it is calendar capacity: an ad that fills Saturday and leaves Tuesday empty has not worked.

Approach

  • Radius targeting tight enough that travel time never becomes the objection
  • Offer built on a first-visit service rather than a discount on everything
  • Creative shot in the chair — result, not premises
  • Booking as the conversion, with weekday slots promoted separately

How it would be judged

  • Cost per booking held against the average first-visit value
  • Rebooking rate at 8 weeks, since a salon lives on the second visit
  • Weekday fill rate as a separate line, never blended into the total
Sample strategy — not a client result

Real estate — sample strategy

Long consideration, high value, and a lead that a human has to call. The media buying question is not how to get leads cheaply — it is how to get leads an agent will still return calls to in week three.

Approach

  • Separate campaigns for browsing intent and viewing intent; never one blended cost per lead
  • Qualification inside the form, accepting a higher cost per lead on purpose
  • Creative that leads with the specific unit, price band and handover date
  • Retargeting sequenced over weeks, matched to how long the decision takes

How it would be judged

  • Cost per qualified lead, defined with the agent before launch
  • Contact rate and viewing rate, reported back from the agent’s CRM
  • Cost per viewing as the real number, with cost per lead treated as diagnostic only
