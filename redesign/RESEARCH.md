# Buyer research — what the market actually says

Phase 3 of the `10k-websites` skill, run 2026-08-30. The point of this file is to
make the site's copy say what buyers already believe, in their words, instead of
what a marketer would like them to believe.

Sources are public industry write-ups and merchant-facing guides. None of it is a
claim about Khalid's own results; it is the language of the market he sells into.

## Who the site talks to

1. Brands and business owners considering his services.
2. Employers and recruiters hiring a media buyer or performance marketer.
3. Agencies open to collaboration.

## What COD e-commerce owners are actually living with

The numbers the market quotes for Morocco and the region:

- 80 to 90 percent of Moroccan online transactions are cash on delivery.
- Order refusal rates run 15 to 30 percent.
- Return to origin reaches 25 to 35 percent.
- Carriers hold the cash 2 to 5 days before it lands, so working capital is needed
  to ship before being paid.

The sentences that matter, because they are the pain in the buyer's own framing:

- "A 30 percent fake order rate means 30 percent of your marketing budget is pure
  waste."
- "Customers place orders without real intent, driven by ads, curiosity, or a
  moment of impulse. Without confirmation, these ship anyway. You pay for
  packaging, courier pickup, and return shipping."
- "Higher confirmation rate and a lower failed-delivery rate from the same
  traffic, the two levers that decide whether COD is profitable."

**This validates Khalid's existing positioning rather than replacing it.** His line
"I find the gap between reported and real" is a precise description of the exact
thing the market says is killing its margins. The site does not need a new hook.
It needs to prove that hook is the buyer's own problem, in the buyer's own numbers.

## Why buyers hesitate to hire

The recurring objections, from write-ups on merchants who hired badly:

- Being burned before. Owners describe being "misled by overpromises" and
  "mis-hiring", costing time, money and opportunity.
- Overpromising. "10x your sales in 30 days" and "guaranteed viral campaigns" are
  named as the exploitative pitches that rarely deliver.
- Slow replies. A response time over 48 hours reads to owners as not caring.
- No explanation. Owners cancel legitimate work because nobody took the time to
  explain it. One analysis of 156 e-commerce relationships found that agencies
  with strong communication delivered 47 percent better results.
- Nobody learning the business before spending.

## What this changes in the build

1. **The hook stays.** "I find the gap between reported and real" is the buyer's
   own problem stated plainly. Keep it verbatim.
2. **The proof section leads with restraint, not size.** Against a market tired of
   "10x in 30 days", the persuasive move is the labelled, honest figure. The
   "as reported in Meta Ads Manager" basis line stops being a disclaimer and
   becomes the selling point: he is the one who tells you which number is real.
3. **The COD worked example earns its place.** It performs the market's own
   arithmetic, and its "not a client result" label is exactly the restraint the
   research says buyers are starving for.
4. **Answer the burned-before objection early**, not buried in the FAQ. The
   existing audit-first answer is the right one and should sit higher.
5. **Say the reply time out loud.** 48 hours is the market's stated threshold, so
   a concrete promise beats a vague one. Needs Khalid's confirmation of what he
   can actually commit to before it ships.
6. **Process before spend.** The five-step process already opens with a
   conversation and a review before any media decision. Research says that is the
   sequence buyers want, so give it visual weight.
7. **No superlatives anywhere.** Every "best", "leading" or "expert" the copy
   might reach for maps directly onto the pitch buyers say burned them.
